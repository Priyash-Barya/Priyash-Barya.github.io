We show that preparing a highly transparent, strongly dispersive medium inside a microring resonator — via spectral hole burning in erbium-doped thin-film lithium niobate — slows the group velocity of light enough to boost the resonator's quality factor by nearly three orders of magnitude, exceeding Q > 10^8. The interplay between the narrowed spectral feature and the underlying bare resonance produces a Fano lineshape that can be dynamically tuned electro-optically, and we develop a theoretical model explaining the observed linewidths, which fall outside standard Bloch-equation behavior. This work introduces a general, chip-scale route to ultra-narrowband, tunable photonic resonances useful for both classical and quantum optical processing.
