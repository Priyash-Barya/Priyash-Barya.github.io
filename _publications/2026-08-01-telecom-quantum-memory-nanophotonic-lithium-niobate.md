We demonstrate an atomic frequency comb (AFC) quantum memory built on erbium-doped thin-film lithium niobate (Er:TFLN), storing single-photon-level telecom-band pulses for more than a microsecond — well beyond what simple propagation delay in even the best nanophotonic waveguides could achieve. We verify the quantum nature of the storage through retrieval-phase coherence and sub-single-photon noise, and show the platform's flexibility by storing up to 20 temporal modes across a 2.2 GHz acceptance bandwidth. These results establish Er:TFLN as a practical, wafer-scale, lithographically defined platform for on-chip quantum memory at telecom wavelengths — a key missing element for photonic quantum networking and quantum repeaters.
