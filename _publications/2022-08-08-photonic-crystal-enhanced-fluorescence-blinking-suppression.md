Quantum dots are attractive single-molecule tags, but their utility for single-unit detection is normally limited by weak, intermittent ("blinking") emission and low collection efficiency from omnidirectional radiation. We show that a photonic crystal (PC) surface amplifies quantum dot emission by nearly 3000-fold through the combined effects of enhanced excitation, directional extraction, and improved quantum efficiency, while simultaneously suppressing blinking — increasing the fraction of time a quantum dot is "on" from 15% to 85%. This enables single quantum dot sensitivity using an inexpensive, low numerical-aperture optical setup, with application to digital-resolution miRNA biomarker sensing.
